​​​ ​Shiori~n!​ ​​

​​​ ​the Odyssey of the Seas.​ ​​
​​​ ​Seven days.​ ​​
​​​ ​One ship.​ ​​

​​​ ​high flying acrobatics,​ ​​
​​​ ​aerial stunts,​ ​​
​​​ ​futuristic drone shows,​ ​​

​​​ ​before wrapping it all up with a visit to​ ​​ ​​ ​one of NASA's legendary launch sites.​ ​​

​​​ ​Room tour!​ ​​

​​​ ​Mine didn't come with a window, though,​ ​​
​​​ ​so I completely lost track of time​ ​​ ​​ ​and slept in most days.​ ​​

​​​ ​And possibly an unwanted guest​ ​​ ​​ ​if you're not careful.​ ​​
​​​ ​The bed was super comfy.​ ​​

​​​ ​Way nicer than my bed at home, to be honest.​ ​​
​​​ ​Behind the wardrobe doors, you'll find a fridge,​ ​​

​​​ ​daily itinerary,​ ​​
​​​ ​and just shelf space if you're​ ​​ ​​ ​the type to actually unpack.​ ​​
​​​ ​I'm not.​ ​​
​​​ ​I just open my suitcase like it's a​ ​​ ​​ ​treasure chest and hunt for loot.​ ​​
​​​ ​The bathroom!​ ​​
​​​ ​Spacious shower...​ ​​
​​​ ​Sanitary disposal bags...​ ​​
​​​ ​Traveler-sized soap, shampoo, conditioner...​ ​​
​​​ ​Fancy shmancy glass cups and extra amenities.​ ​​

​​​ ​dance classes,​ ​​
​​​ ​game nights, spa treatments,​ ​​ ​​ ​and fitness appointments.​ ​​

​​​ ​but it's smaller than the one​ ​​ ​​ ​on the last ship I was on.​ ​​
​​​ ​The whole ship felt a bit older and smaller.​ ​​

​​​ ​A pub,​ ​​
​​​ ​two lounges,​ ​​
​​​ ​some merch stores,​ ​​
​​​ ​and a mix of luxury shops selling​ ​​ ​​ ​perfumes, watches, and jewelry.​ ​​
​​​ ​There were also smaller stalls​ ​​ ​​ ​with more affordable jewelry​ ​​
​​​ ​and the usual souvenir stuff.​ ​​

​​​ ​For gacha addicts.​ ​​

​​​ ​There's a towel draped over your back,​ ​​
​​​ ​and they play this low ambient​ ​​ ​​ ​music around the room.​ ​​
​​​ ​The lights were dim and they give you an option​ ​​ ​​ ​of what type of scent you want in the air.​ ​​
​​​ ​And I chose a full body massage,​ ​​
​​​ ​which covers everything from my lower legs​ ​​ ​​ ​and thighs, to my back and shoulders.​ ​​

​​​ ​and after the massage,​ ​​ ​​ ​they give you recommendations​ ​​
​​​ ​on which part of the body you should work on.​ ​​
​​​ ​For example, they told me that I could get​ ​​ ​​ ​this type of oil for this area,​ ​​
​​​ ​and you should try to massage it more.​ ​​
​​​ ​And I'm like, yeah...​ ​​
​​​ ​I'm never gonna see this again.​ ​​
​​​ ​After the massage, aboard the ship​ ​​ ​​ ​are several specialty restaurants,​ ​​
​​​ ​and Izumi was a hibachi spot we'd pass by​ ​​ ​​ ​constantly on the way to the pool deck.​ ​​
​​​ ​For those who don't know,​ ​​
​​​ ​hibachi restaurants are dine-in areas​ ​​
​​​ ​where the chef cooks right in front of you.​ ​​
​​​ ​It's an interactive experience.​ ​​
​​​ ​Maybe a little chaotic since they might​ ​​ ​​ ​toss a shrimp or egg your way.​ ​​
​​​ ​And if your reflexes aren't that great,​ ​​ ​​ ​it might be a bit daunting.​ ​​
​​​ ​The chef was pretty fun.​ ​​
​​​ ​He'd flip an egg into the air​ ​​ ​​ ​with a spatula without cracking it,​ ​​
​​​ ​roll it across his hat,​ ​​
​​​ ​and then he would juggle the spatulas.​ ​​
​​​ ​The food was solid. Nice and savory.​ ​​
​​​ ​I'd say overall, it's...​ ​​ ​​ ​an entertaining experience,​ ​​
​​​ ​but if you're someone who prefers quieter meals,​ ​​ ​​ ​this would not be the place for you.​ ​​
​​​ ​But if you have children or​ ​​ ​​ ​enjoy a bit of excitement,​ ​​
​​​ ​it might be worth to give it a try.​ ​​
​​​ ​Just not aboard a cruise ship.​ ​​
​​​ ​A specialty restaurant like this was not cheap here.​ ​​
​​​ ​For a family this size, you're probably looking​ ​​ ​​ ​at 600 bucks for the full meal and show.​ ​​
​​​ ​So yeah, just to let you know.​ ​​
​​​ ​Ever heard of NASA?​ ​​
​​​ ​It's America's main research agency​ ​​
​​​ ​dedicated to exploring space and​ ​​ ​​ ​everything beyond the stars.​ ​​
​​​ ​In the gator-infested wetlands of Florida,​ ​​
​​​ ​NASA operates one of its major field centers:​ ​​ ​​ ​the Kennedy Space Center,​ ​​
​​​ ​which serves as their primary launch site.​ ​​
​​​ ​When you first walk in,​ ​​ ​​ ​you'll see space-themed trash cans​ ​​
​​​ ​and ticket booths,​ ​​ ​​ ​which I found kind of cute.​ ​​
​​​ ​Inside, you'll find historical exhibits,​ ​​
​​​ ​short documentary clips,​ ​​
​​​ ​and replicas or real artifacts​ ​​ ​​ ​from past space missions.​ ​​
​​​ ​In the Apollo/Saturn V Center,​ ​​ ​​ ​you can get a front row seat​ ​​
​​​ ​into a recreated launch control room.​ ​​
​​​ ​From here, you can experience what it was like​ ​​ ​​ ​for operators to prepare for takeoff.​ ​​
​​​ ​It comes complete with (I think)​ ​​ ​​ ​original equipment,​ ​​
​​​ ​countdown clocks,​ ​​
​​​ ​and a dramatic presentation​ ​​ ​​ ​that simulates a launch.​ ​​
​​​ ​Once you've finished the presentation,​ ​​
​​​ ​you'll be able to freely wander into the next room​ ​​
​​​ ​where this massive rocket is located.​ ​​
​​​ ​This right here holds the title of​ ​​ ​​ ​the largest rocket ever flown​ ​​
​​​ ​and stands at a whopping 363 feet,​ ​​ ​​ ​or 111 meters tall.​ ​​
​​​ ​Nearby, you'll find displays like Apollo 14's​ ​​ ​​ ​command module, nicknamed Kitty Hawk.​ ​​
​​​ ​Oddly cute for what it is.​ ​​
​​​ ​For those who don't know,​ ​​ ​​ ​the Apollo spacecraft has two components:​ ​​
​​​ ​the Service Module and Command Module.​ ​​
​​​ ​The Service one handles life support, power,​ ​​ ​​ ​and propulsion while in space,​ ​​
​​​ ​while the Command one houses the living quarters​ ​​
​​​ ​complete with mission computers​ ​​ ​​ ​and navigation systems.​ ​​
​​​ ​The Service Module is the part that​ ​​ ​​ ​detaches when it returns to Earth,​ ​​
​​​ ​so Kitty Hawk here is the only part of​ ​​ ​​ ​the spacecraft to return from the mission.​ ​​
​​​ ​But yeah, there's just so much​ ​​ ​​ ​tech on display here.​ ​​
​​​ ​Just look at the complexity of this rocket engine.​ ​​
​​​ ​Layers of wiring and piping​ ​​ ​​ ​that makes my brain hurt.​ ​​
​​​ ​Then there's the retired Space Shuttle Atlantis,​ ​​
​​​ ​which flew 33 missions over 26 years.​ ​​
​​​ ​To top it off, you can experience a simulated​ ​​ ​​ ​launch in the Shuttle flight simulator.​ ​​
​​​ ​After a safety briefing and info about G-force,​ ​​
​​​ ​you're strapped into a launch seat.​ ​​
​​​ ​The room begins to rumble intensely​ ​​ ​​ ​as the shuttle tilts back,​ ​​
​​​ ​and then you "launch" into space,​ ​​
​​​ ​ending with a view of Earth from orbit.​ ​​
​​​ ​Wave goodbye to the external tank,​ ​​
​​​ ​and say hello to outer space.​ ​​
​​​ ​And that was the peak experience​ ​​ ​​ ​of my cruise ship vacation.​ ​​

​​​ ​taking a freezing swim,​ ​​
​​​ ​to ending it all with a full tour​ ​​ ​​ ​through space history...​ ​​
​​​ ​Yeah. One heck of a trip.​ ​​
